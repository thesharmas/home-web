China's oil tactics have been clever. Now the costs are mounting

China's response to the Hormuz crisis initially showed impressive agility—cutting crude imports and building fuel stocks—but a subsequent surge in refined-product exports has drained domestic inventories and distorted global oil markets. The scramble for crude has pushed Brent back to $100 a barrel and driven tanker rates to records, while retail price controls and teapot-refiner stress are squeezing domestic supply. Beijing now faces a painful trilemma: allow higher retail fuel prices, subsidise loss-making state refiners, or draw down strategic crude reserves further.

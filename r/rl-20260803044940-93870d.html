A common European safe asset is still unlikely

The article examines why a common European safe asset—a shared EU benchmark bond—remains unlikely despite growing EU debt issuance. High national debt levels in countries like France, Italy, and Spain, combined with the absence of a fiscal and political union, make meaningful debt mutualisation politically and economically fraught. EU bonds continue to trade at a discount to German Bunds due to limited size, lack of permanence, and thin liquidity, leaving deeper European financial integration without a shared sovereign benchmark.

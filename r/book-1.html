1873: The Rothschilds, the First Great Depression, and the Making of the Modern World — Liaquat Ahamed

The book examines the financial crash of 1873, tracing how the first era of globalization and a speculative railroad-bond boom—largely financed through the Rothschild banking family—collapsed into a worldwide crisis of defaults, currency chaos, and prolonged deflation. Liaquat Ahamed shows how the crash rippled into major historical events, including the end of Reconstruction in the United States and the decline of the Ottoman Empire, while also fueling a rise in antisemitic scapegoating of Jewish financiers.

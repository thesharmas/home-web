Urban life rests on invisible, extraordinary technology

A review of Jonn Elledge's "31 Inventions that Built Our World," which explores the overlooked technological innovations underpinning modern urban life, from sewers and elevators to house numbering and windows. The book blends obscure trivia with broader arguments about how incremental progress has made once-remarkable inventions invisible through their ubiquity. The reviewer finds the book informative and often funny, praising its digressive, chapter-by-chapter tour of city-shaping technologies.

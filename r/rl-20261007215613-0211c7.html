How US mortgage bonds can trigger a 'vicious loop' for Treasury yields

The article explains how US agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS), a ~$7tn market, have 'negative convexity' that forces portfolio managers to sell bonds precisely when markets are already falling, amplifying Treasury yield spikes. As yields rise, MBS duration increases, compelling holders to offload Treasuries to maintain their target risk levels, creating a self-reinforcing 'vicious loop' of selling.

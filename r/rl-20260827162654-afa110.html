What a century of data tells us about today's corporate bond spreads

Duncan Lamont of Schroders examines a century of data to argue that today's tight corporate bond spreads may not be as anomalous as a 30-year view suggests, drawing parallels with the post-WWII era of persistently low spreads. He identifies five historical factors that suppressed spreads then—high government debt, strong growth and low defaults, financial repression, institutional demand, and defensive sector composition—and assesses how many apply today.

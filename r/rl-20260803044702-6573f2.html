Border walls are symbols of power—and monuments to delusion

The article uses a walk along Hadrian's Wall as a springboard to reflect on the nature of border walls as symbols of imperial power that ultimately become monuments to delusion. Drawing on history and the site of Vindolanda, the piece humanises the Roman occupation beyond military might, touching on everyday life. It concludes that grandiose walls—from Hadrian's to the Berlin Wall—tend to crumble, be scavenged, or become tourist attractions rather than enduring as the barriers their builders intended.

Technology and the Rise of Great Powers

Jeffrey Ding's book presents a novel theory of how technological revolutions shape great power competition, arguing that success depends not on who first innovates but on which states best diffuse and adapt new technologies at scale. Drawing on historical case studies of the First, Second, and Third Industrial Revolutions and statistical analysis, Ding develops a framework centered on institutional adaptations and skill infrastructure. The book's findings have direct implications for understanding how AI could influence the current US-China power balance.

The Debt-Equity Distinction: A Century of Policy by Accident

The article argues that the tax deductibility of corporate interest payments—which makes debt systematically cheaper than equity—was not a deliberate policy choice but an accidental byproduct of World War I financing exigencies that was never reversed. This debt-equity distinction has profoundly shaped modern finance, enabling leveraged buyouts, debt-funded share buybacks, and debt-heavy M&A while disadvantaging equity-dependent innovators and increasing systemic financial fragility.

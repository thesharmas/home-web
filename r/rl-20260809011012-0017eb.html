The carry trade that can't be unwound

The piece argues that Japan's public sector has effectively become the world's largest currency carry trader, borrowing short-term at below-market rates via Bank of Japan reserves and investing in foreign equities and bonds without hedging currency risk. As the BoJ attempts to normalize interest rates, it faces a dilemma: raising rates inflicts massive losses on the public sector's balance sheet (given ~93% of GDP in floating-rate liabilities), while not raising rates risks further yen depreciation and rising inflation.

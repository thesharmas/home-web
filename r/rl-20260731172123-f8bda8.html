Physicists Solve a Big Quantum Mystery. Now, Old Results Don't Add Up.

For 25 years, physicists debated why the muon's measured magnetic wobble (g-2) differed from theoretical predictions, hinting at unknown particles. New lattice QCD calculations resolved that discrepancy by matching experimental results, but they now conflict with older data-driven predictions based on electron-positron collision experiments. A new pion production measurement from the VEPP-2000 collider in Siberia dramatically diverges from past results, leaving physicists uncertain whether experimental errors or genuinely new physics are responsible.
